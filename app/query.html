Data Retention Under DPDP Act 2023

Under Section 8(7) of the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023, a Data Fiduciary must not retain personal data beyond the period necessary for the purpose for which it was collected or processed.

Key requirements:

  • No fixed minimum or maximum retention period is mandated in the Act itself
  • Retention must be limited to what is necessary for the stated purpose
  • Once the purpose is fulfilled, data must be erased or anonymised
  • A Data Principal can withdraw consent, triggering immediate deletion obligations
  • The Data Protection Board may specify retention limits for specific data categories via future rules

Practical steps for compliance:

  • Document a Retention Schedule for each data category you hold
  • Implement automated deletion or anonymisation workflows
  • Include retention periods explicitly in your Privacy Policy (this is required)
  • For health and financial data, cross-check with sector-specific rules (IRDAI, RBI)
  • Train your team on the deletion protocol when consent is withdrawn

⚠️ Important: Where Indian tax or labour laws mandate longer retention (e.g., 8 years for income tax records, 5 years for PF records), those obligations override DPDP — you retain for compliance, but restrict further processing.

Employee Data Under DPDP Act

Yes — employee data is covered under the DPDP Act 2023, though with some nuances introduced by Section 17(2)(b).

Current position:

  • The Central Government may exempt certain employer–employee data processing from specific provisions, but no such exemption has been notified yet
  • Until an exemption is notified, treat employee data with full DPDP obligations
  • Employees are "Data Principals" and have the same rights as customers (access, correction, erasure, grievance redressal)

Mandatory retention periods for employee records (overriding laws):

  • Payroll / salary records: 8 years (Income Tax Act, 1961)
  • PF / ESI records: 5 years minimum (EPF & MP Act, 1952)
  • Leave records: 3 years (Shops & Establishments Act)
  • Contract of employment: Duration of employment + 3 years
  • POSH records: 3 years post complaint resolution

For records where the retention period has elapsed and no legal obligation applies, you must delete or anonymise. Keeping ex-employee data "just in case" is a DPDP violation.
